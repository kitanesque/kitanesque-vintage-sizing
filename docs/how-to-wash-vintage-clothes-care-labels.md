Of the 39 cashmere care labels we have read, 11 say hand wash (9 with no temperature, 2 with one), 12 allow a machine wash, 5 say dry clean only or do not wash. 25 say dry flat and 6 say do not tumble dry. ## How to wash vintage cashmere: what 39 labels say

Of the 39 cashmere care labels we have read, 11 say hand wash (9 with no temperature, 2 with one), 12 allow a machine wash, 5 say dry clean only or do not wash. 25 say dry flat and 6 say do not tumble dry.

- **Dry flat** (25 labels): lay it out flat to dry rather than hanging it, so the weight of the water does not stretch it
- **Wash inside out** (12 labels): turn it inside out to protect the surface, the print or the embroidery
- **Wash at 30°** (9 labels): machine wash at 30 °C, the cool cycle
- **Hand wash, no temperature** (9 labels): by hand in cool water, not in the machine

On our cashmere pieces we print: Hand wash cold with a wool wash, press the water out in a towel and dry flat. Fold it rather than hang it.

## Wool

Of the 19 wool care labels we have read, 1 says hand wash (with a temperature), 4 allow a machine wash, 9 say dry clean only or do not wash. 2 say dry flat and 4 say do not tumble dry.

- **Do not bleach** (7 labels): no chlorine or oxygen bleach; it strips colour and weakens the fibre
- **Dry clean only** (7 labels): professional dry cleaning only, no water at home
- **Iron low** (5 labels): cool iron only, the one-dot setting
- **Do not tumble dry** (4 labels): air dry; dryer heat shrinks and felts the fibre

On our wool pieces we print: Hand wash cold or on the wool cycle, dry flat and fold rather than hang. No tumble dryer. On tailored wool: Dry clean. Brush after wearing, hang on a shaped hanger and steam out creases.

## Silk

Of the 3 silk care labels we have read, 1 says dry clean only or do not wash. 1 says dry flat. A small sample so far; the counts grow as pieces come in.

- **Do not bleach** (3 labels): no chlorine or oxygen bleach; it strips colour and weakens the fibre
- **Do not wash** (1 label): no water at all; dry clean or specialist clean
- **Gentle dry clean** (1 label): dry clean on a gentle cycle
- **Dry flat** (1 label): lay it out flat to dry rather than hanging it, so the weight of the water does not stretch it

On our silk pieces we print: Hand wash cold and briefly with a mild detergent, or dry clean. Don’t soak or wring; dry flat in the shade; iron low on the reverse.

## Cotton and denim

Of the 51 cotton and denim care labels we have read, 4 say hand wash (all with no temperature), 24 allow a machine wash. 1 says dry flat and 5 say do not tumble dry.

- **Machine wash** (19 labels): machine washable on a normal cycle unless a temperature is given
- **Do not bleach** (17 labels): no chlorine or oxygen bleach; it strips colour and weakens the fibre
- **Wash with similar colours** (16 labels): the dye can run in the wash, so keep it with like colours
- **Iron low** (14 labels): cool iron only, the one-dot setting

On our cotton and denim pieces we print: Wash at 30–40° with similar colours, hang to dry and iron medium. On jeans: Wash at 30° inside out with similar colours and hang to dry. Skip the tumble dryer to keep the stretch and the fit.

## Questions about washing vintage clothes

### Can I machine wash vintage cashmere?

Only when the label says so. On the 39 cashmere labels we have read, 11 say hand wash (9 with no temperature, 2 with one), 12 allow a machine wash and 5 say dry clean only; 25 say dry flat. On our cashmere pieces we print: Hand wash cold with a wool wash, press the water out in a towel and dry flat. Fold it rather than hang it. The label on the piece wins over any general rule.

### Does "dry clean only" really mean it?

We treat it as it reads. 21 of the 179 labels we have read say dry clean only, most of them on wool (7), cashmere (5), synthetics (4). On tailored wool and lined pieces our own care line says: Dry clean. Brush after wearing, hang on a shaped hanger and steam out creases.

### Why do so many labels say "wash with similar colours" or "wash inside out"?

Because the dye can run and the surface can rub. 44 labels say wash with similar colours and 39 say wash inside out; on cotton and denim alone the two lines appear 16 and 13 times (29 of the 51 cotton pieces are jeans or denim). Inside out keeps the surface, the print and the embroidery away from the drum; similar colours keeps a bleeding dye off lighter pieces.

### How do I dry a jumper flat?

41 labels say dry flat, 27 of them on cashmere and wool. Flat means laid out on a towel, pushed back into shape, away from direct heat; hanging a wet knit stretches it under its own weight. On our wool knits we print: Hand wash cold or on the wool cycle, dry flat and fold rather than hang. No tumble dryer.

### What does "do not bleach" mean on a vintage label?

It is the most common line on the labels we have read: 54 of 179. It means no chlorine and no oxygen bleach; both strip colour and weaken the fibre, and on an old piece they also lift the age marks unevenly.
