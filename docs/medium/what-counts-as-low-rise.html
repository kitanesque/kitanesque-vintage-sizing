What Counts as Low-Rise? Rise Measured on Vintage Jeans

On the 134 pairs of jeans we have measured flat, the front rise runs from 6″ to 12.5″ with a median of 8.5″; 57 of them (43 %) sit at 8″ or under, which is what we call low-rise.

Our working bands, on the front rise measured flat from the crotch seam to the top of the waistband: low under 8.5″ (8″ or under on a tape read to the half inch), with ultra-low under 7″ as the lowest part of it; mid 8.5–10.5″; high over 10.5″. The same bands as our fit checker and size guide. Brands name the same measurement differently on the label and in their charts; the tape is what we go by. Our Low-rise jeans category collects every pair at 8″ or under, plus the pairs the label itself calls low-rise.

Questions about rise

Is an 8-inch rise low-rise?

Yes, on our tape. Our low band is everything under 8.5″, so on a tape read to the half inch an 8″ front rise is the top of it: of the 134 pairs of jeans we have measured flat, 57 sit at 8″ or under and go into our Low-rise jeans category. At 8.5″ a pair reads as mid-rise here, mid runs to 10.5″, and over 10.5″ is high. The median across everything we have measured is 8.5″. Brands name the same measurement differently on the label, so we go by the tape, not the chart.

What rise did Y2K jeans have?

On the 75 pairs tagged Y2K that we have measured, the median front rise is 8.5″; 34 of them sit at 8″ or under and the lowest is 6″. The 3 pairs tagged 90s on our rail sit higher, with a median of 11.5″, though that is too few to call a rule. Our tape, laid flat, not the brand's chart.

How do I measure the rise?

Lay the jeans flat and buttoned, then measure the front from the crotch seam, where the four seams meet, straight up to the top of the waistband. That is the front rise, the number on every pair we list; the back rise is measured the same way on the back. A brand's size chart is not always measured this way, so compare tape with tape: measure a pair you own and read it against the numbers on this page.

Does low-rise mean a smaller waist?

No. A low rise puts the waistband lower, on the hips, where the body is wider, so the waistband measures more than the number on the label, not less. On our low and ultra-low pairs the waistband measures on average 1.6″ more than the label number; on our mid and high pairs, 0.5″ more. The label waist tells you the size the pair was cut for; the waistband measurement in the listing tells you where it will actually sit.

Are low-rise jeans back?

We cannot speak for fashion, but we can count. Of the 134 pairs of jeans we have measured, 57 sit at 8″ or under, and 36 of those are in stock right now. Most of them date from the Y2K years: 34 of the 75 Y2K pairs we have measured are low or ultra-low. Every pair on this page links to its listing, with the full measurements.
