Vintage Dress Sizing by Brand: Label vs Tape

On the 28 woven vintage and second-hand dresses we have measured, from 44 labels, the bust on the tape came out right on the UK standard for the size on the listing on average: 25% ran small (more than 1″ under), 36% were true to size (within 1″) and 39% ran large (more than 1″ over). Where the waist was measured too (17 dresses), it came out +1″ against the standard. A further 27 stretch or wrap dresses (bodycon, ribbed knit, wrap fronts) are in the in-stock list and the data but not in the comparison: a stretch dress measures well under the body it is made to stretch over and a wrap has no fixed bust, so for those the size on the listing is the guide.

Among the labels we have measured two or more times, Whistles came out roomiest in the bust against the UK standard for the size we listed it as (+3.5″ on average) and Zara tightest (0″). Labels with two or more measured dresses get their own row. “Listed as” is the UK size on each dress’s own listing, judged from its flat measurements, never a calculation from the old label. “vs UK standard” is how much bigger or smaller the dress measured than the standard body measurement for that listed size; a dress that is meant to skim the body measures a little over it. Questions about vintage dress sizes

Do vintage dresses run small?

Not as a rule. Across the 28 woven dresses we have measured, the bust came out 0″ against the UK standard for the size we listed on average; 7 of them (25%) measured more than an inch under the standard, 10 (36%) were within an inch and 11 (39%) more than an inch over. Stretch dresses are left out of that count: measured flat they read far under the body they are made for, so the listed size is the guide there. Among the labels we have measured two or more times, Whistles came out roomiest in the bust against the UK standard for the size we listed it as (+3.5″ on average) and Zara tightest (0″).

What bust is a UK 10 vintage dress?

On the 3 dresses we listed as UK 10, the bust measured 35–38″ round, against a UK standard of about 35″ for that size; the average came out +1.5″. We size each dress by its flat measurements, not by the number sewn into it, so a dress listed as UK 10 is meant to fit a UK 10 today.

What bust is a UK 12 vintage dress?

On the 5 dresses we listed as UK 12, the bust measured 37–40″ round, against a UK standard of about 37″ for that size; the average came out +1.5″. We size each dress by its flat measurements, not by the number sewn into it, so a dress listed as UK 12 is meant to fit a UK 12 today.

Is a 1990s size 12 the same as a size 12 today?

Not reliably, which is why we list by the tape, not by the label. Our dresses by decade, bust against the UK standard for the size we listed: 80s +0.5″ (2 dresses); Y2K 0″ (5 dresses); 90s −3″ (3 dresses). The number on an old label is only a starting point; the bust and waist measurements on each listing are what tell you whether it fits.

Do Coast dresses run small or true to size?

On the 3 Coast dresses we measured, the bust came out +0.5″ against the UK standard for the size we listed on average, which we would call true to size; the waist, on the 1 we could measure, came out +0.5″. Measured flat on each dress, not the brand's own chart.

How do you measure a dress?

Laid flat and unstretched: bust straight across under the arms and doubled, waist across the narrowest point and doubled, hips across the widest point of the skirt where the cut has one, and length from the shoulder seam (or the top of the bodice on a strapless dress) to the hem. Inches, on the dress itself; the UK standard we compare against is the same women's table the site uses for Will it fit?.
